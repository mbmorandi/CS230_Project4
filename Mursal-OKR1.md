# Mursal’s Project 3 — OKR: Responsible Sharing and Copyright Protection

## StudentShare Company Alignment

StudentShare is a student-centered platform that makes file sharing convenient, safe, and accessible without overwhelming users. Its purpose is to help students share useful study resources, organize files, and collaborate more easily.

StudentShare’s mission is to provide an easy-to-use file-sharing tool without sacrificing user safety, privacy, fairness, or the quality of the user experience. The company values convenience, safety and responsibility, privacy, fairness, and accessibility.

StudentShare is not designed to promise complete anonymity. Instead, it aims to reduce unnecessary login barriers and make access simple for students. Authentication may occur behind the scenes to support security, responsible use, reporting, and appeals. Students should have control over which files and folders they share and whether they choose to use optional synchronization features.

## Objective

Encourage ethical sharing of documents while safeguarding intellectual-property rights, privacy, and student control over the items they share.

StudentShare is intended to allow students to share their own notes, public-domain documents, and any resources they have permission to share. However, the site must also discourage users from posting copyrighted textbook pages, lecture recordings, answer keys, plagiarized work, or other material they do not have permission to distribute. Copyright is legal protection for original works of authorship, such as books, movies, music, and software [1].

This objective supports the StudentShare values of safety, responsibility, privacy, fairness, and convenience. The company should make responsible behavior easy to understand without making the platform difficult to use.

## Key Results

1. Before a student uploads or shares a document, 100% of upload and sharing screens will display a clear reminder about copyright, privacy, and academic-integrity responsibilities.

2. At least 90% of copyright, privacy, or responsible-sharing reports will receive an acknowledgment or initial response within 48 hours.

3. In a usability test with 20 students, at least 80% of participants will correctly identify which materials they may share and successfully choose an appropriate access setting for a file or folder.

4. At least 80% of students who complete the usability test will rate the sharing guidance as clear and easy to understand on a survey scale of 1 to 10.

## Metrics and Experiment

StudentShare will track the number of uploads, shared links, reports, response times, restricted content, appeals, and appeal outcomes. The company will also measure whether students understand sharing permissions and privacy controls.

The main experiment will be a usability test with 20 students who represent the platform’s target users. Participants will read the upload rules and use a StudentShare prototype to complete several tasks. For example, they will decide whether they may share:

- Their own class notes
- A full textbook chapter
- A lecture recording
- An answer key for a quiz or exam
- A study guide created with permission
- A file containing another student’s private information

Participants will also choose an access setting for each file. For example, they may choose to share with a small study group, share with a class, or keep the file private. The study will measure task completion, correct answers, time required, participant confidence, and feedback about the instructions.

Usability testing helps organizations learn whether representative users can understand and effectively use a product [2]. If fewer than 80% of participants correctly identify permitted content or choose a suitable sharing setting, StudentShare will improve its interface, upload guidance, and privacy instructions before expanding the service.

## Ethical Impacts and Concerns

One major concern is copyright infringement. Students might upload textbook pages, paid study materials, lecture recordings, or other copyrighted content without permission. This could negatively affect authors, publishers, instructors, and educational institutions.

A second concern is academic integrity. Students could misuse the service to distribute quiz answers, exam answers, plagiarized notes, or unauthorized assignment materials. StudentShare must make clear that convenient sharing does not mean that every file is appropriate to upload.

A third concern is privacy. Students may accidentally upload documents containing names, grades, email addresses, student identification numbers, or other personal information. Privacy controls must be understandable, and students must know who can access their files.

A fourth concern is fairness. An automated detection system may incorrectly flag original notes or authorized materials. Removing content automatically without an explanation or appeal could unfairly limit students’ access to legitimate resources.

## Ethical Safeguards

StudentShare will provide clear rules before every upload or sharing action. The platform will explain that users should share only their own work, public-domain materials, or materials they have permission to distribute.

The platform will include sharing controls so students can decide whether a document is private, limited to a selected group, or shared more broadly. StudentShare will minimize the retention of unflagged shared content and will allow users to choose which folders, if any, are included in optional synchronization.

Copyright owners, instructors, and students will be able to report concerns. StudentShare will acknowledge or respond to most reports within 48 hours. When a system flags possible copyrighted or inappropriate content, the platform will explain why it was flagged and provide an appeal process.

When possible, a person will review flagged content before a final removal decision. This safeguard recognizes that automated systems can make errors. Students whose content is restricted will have an opportunity to explain whether they own the material or have permission to share it.

StudentShare will also remind users not to upload private information. Repeated serious violations may result in warnings or account restrictions, but enforcement should be fair, consistent, and explained to the user. These safeguards allow StudentShare to support convenient collaboration while respecting creators, students, and educational institutions.

These safeguards also support the ACM Code of Ethics by reducing adverse impacts, respecting privacy, and respecting the work required to create original materials [3].

## Conclusion

StudentShare should be convenient and useful for students to share files, while also respecting privacy, academic integrity, and the rights of creators. The platform should give students clear directions before they share documents, allow them to decide who can access their files, and provide a fair process for reporting concerns and submitting appeals.

My OKR supports StudentShare’s purpose by balancing convenience and accountability. StudentShare can help students collaborate by implementing copyright warnings, privacy controls, timely responses to reports, usability testing, and, when possible, human review. These measures can reduce unauthorized sharing without unfairly restricting approved materials.
