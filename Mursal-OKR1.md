# Mursal’s Project 3 — OKR 1 for StudentShare

## Objective

Promote responsible document sharing while protecting intellectual-property rights.

StudentShare will help students find and share useful academic resources. However, the platform must prevent students from uploading copyrighted materials, private information, or content that violates academic-integrity rules. Copyright protects original creative works, including books, music, movies, and software [1]. StudentShare should support legal sharing of original student notes and authorized materials while respecting the rights of creators and schools.

## Key Results

1. Before a student uploads a document, 100% of uploads will show a clear copyright and academic-integrity reminder.

2. At least 90% of copyright or academic-integrity reports will receive an initial response within 48 hours.

3. In a usability test with 20 students, at least 80% of participants will correctly identify which materials they may legally upload after reading the upload guidance.

## Metrics and Experiment

StudentShare will track the number of uploads, reports, response times, removed content, appeals, and appeal outcomes. These measurements will show whether the platform is responding responsibly and fairly.

To test the upload guidance, StudentShare will conduct a usability test with 20 students. Each participant will read the upload rules and complete a short set of tasks. For example, they will decide whether they may upload:

- Their own class notes
- A full textbook chapter
- A lecture recording
- An answer key for a quiz or exam
- A study guide created with permission

The test will measure whether students can understand the rules, complete the task independently, and explain why an upload is allowed or not allowed. Usability testing uses representative users to identify whether a design is understandable and easy to use [2]. StudentShare will revise its rules and warnings if fewer than 80% of participants answer correctly.

## Ethical Issues

StudentShare could be misused to share copyrighted textbook pages, lecture recordings, plagiarized notes, or answers to quizzes and exams. These actions could violate copyright law and academic-integrity policies.

Another issue is that an automated copyright system may make mistakes. It could flag or block original student notes by accident. This could be unfair and prevent students from sharing lawful materials. Privacy is also important because students may accidentally upload documents containing names, grades, email addresses, or other personal information.

## Ethical Safeguards

StudentShare will give students clear upload rules and a reminder before every upload. The reminder will explain that students should upload only their own work, public-domain materials, or materials they have permission to share.

The platform will provide a reporting system for copyright owners, instructors, and students. A report will receive an initial response within 48 hours. When possible, a person will review content flagged by an automated system before making a final removal decision.

Students will be told why their content was flagged and will have a fair appeal process if they believe the decision was incorrect. Repeated violations may result in warnings or account restrictions. StudentShare will also remind users not to upload private information and will collect only the information necessary to operate the service.

These safeguards support StudentShare’s goal of making academic collaboration easier while respecting creators, students, and educational institutions.
